# UFLI Foundations: what we follow, and where we are in it

Researched 2026-10-13 from UFLI's own free materials (links at the bottom). Read this instead of
answering from memory. Only the order of concepts, the lesson routine and which irregular words arrive
when are recorded here, in our words; no lesson content is copied, and none should be.

## The shape of the programme

- 148 lesson plans: 10 "Getting Ready" lessons (A to J: capital letter names and how to write them),
  128 numbered concept lessons, 10 reviews.
- Suggested kindergarten year: Getting Ready, then lessons 1 to about 68 (longer words start at 63,
  "at the very end" of the kindergarten sequence). First grade begins by going back over short vowels (35 to 41).
- Each lesson is taught over two days, about 30 minutes each.
- UFLI covers decoding only. It leaves rhyme out on purpose (no evidence it helps reading) and leaves
  comprehension to another programme.

Two things in the alphabet unit that are easy to miss:

- **Blends start early.** The sample words for lesson 22 already end in two consonants (CVCC), and the
  "part 2" lessons for r (25) and l (27) are about r and l *inside blends* (CCVC), not more of the same letter.
- **Word chains start at lesson 5** and are in every lesson after: change one letter, read or spell the new word.

## The eight steps of a lesson

| # | Step | Day | Min | What happens | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Phonemic awareness | 1 | 2 | Spoken only. Blend sounds said one at a time into a word; then break a word into its sounds | sound ↔ sound |
| 2 | Visual drill | 1 | 3 | See a grapheme, say its name and its sound. Speed is the point | letter → sound |
| 3 | Auditory drill | 1 | 5 | Hear a sound, write its grapheme, saying it | sound → letter |
| 4 | Blending drill | 1 | 5 | The teacher builds words from what is already known, changing a letter at a time; children sound out and read each | read |
| 5 | New concept | 1, and 3 min on 2 | 15 | The new thing explained: how the sound is made, where the grapheme goes, how the letter is written; then guided reading and spelling | both |
| 6 | Word work | 2 | 6 | Word chains with letter tiles, alternating "make this word" (spell) and "change this letter: what is it now?" (read) | both |
| 7 | Irregular words | 2 | 6 | Read and spell the "heart words" so far; some lessons add new ones | both |
| 8 | Connected text | 2 | 15 | Read sentences, spell at least one sentence, read a short decodable passage | both |

Steps 1 to 4 warm up and review old material; 5 is the only new teaching; 6 to 8 apply it.

## Irregular ("heart") words

UFLI's method: do not memorise the word by sight. Map each letter to its sound as usual and mark only
the letter or letters that do not behave, which are the part learned by heart. Many are irregular only
for now: "the" at lesson 1 has two odd parts, and after th is taught (46) only the e is.

New irregular words by lesson, from the home practice sheets (which begin at lesson 5). A star is the
sheets' own mark; they do not say what it means, but every starred word becomes regular once later
lessons are taught, so it reads as "irregular only for now".

## Where the game is

Lessons 1 to 34 of 128, on six islands: the whole alphabet unit. Next is the review of short vowels with longer words (35 to 41), then digraphs (42 on).

| Island | UFLI | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `i1a1` | 1–5 | |
| `i1a2` | 6–10 | 10 is the checkpoint |
| `i1a3` | 12–14 | 11 skipped: a pronunciation point, not a new correspondence |
| `i1a4` | 15–19 | 19 is the checkpoint |
| `i2a1` | 20–27 | 25 (`i2a1-r2`) teaches CCVC with r after another consonant; 27 (`i2a1-l2`) CCVC with l, and CVCC. Both were added after the island was out: see below |
| `i2a2` | 28–34 | w j y x qu v z, one lesson each. The checkpoint is ours and a boss: the alphabet ends here |
| next | 35–41 | review of each short vowel, with CCVCC and CCCVC words |

Blends, as the game has them:

- Lesson 25 teaches `blend:ccvc` and lesson 27 teaches it again with l, and `blend:cvcc`. UFLI's lesson 22 already
  shows CVCC words; here a word of a shape not yet taught is sounded out and heard but never rebuilt, so from 22 on
  a child meets "desk" that way and is first asked to build one at 27. We put CVCC at 27 because that is where l
  stands before a last consonant (milk, help); r cannot (it changes the vowel: UFLI 77 on).
- The words with two consonants together all have k, h, r or l in them, so none can turn up before island 5.
- A lesson's words are shared between its shapes, and the first of each shape is shown how. With no clip for any
  word of its shape yet, a blend lesson is letter review only: it plays, and teaches nothing. Record before release.
- Saved games from before the two lessons existed: beating an area's checkpoint settles every node in it
  (`settled` in `src/learn/curriculum.ts`), so a child who had finished the island keeps it finished, knows both
  shapes (shown how the first time one comes up) and finds two more little ones at home. A child still on the
  island does 25 and 27 before the checkpoint. The two new little ones are numbered after the island's others
  (`late`), so nobody already woken changes.

The last letters, as the game has them:

- **qu is one grapheme**: one tile, one sound (/kw/), one skill (`gpc:qu`), and never a q or its u alone. A word is cut
  into graphemes by `splitGraphemes` (`src/learn/skills.ts`), which takes the longest grapheme of more than one letter
  that some lesson teaches (`multiGraphemes` in the curriculum), so the digraphs of 42 on need only their lessons; a dot
  in `words.json` forces a cut where that would be wrong. Two letters have no name, so qu is met with "Together, these
  letters make the sound" in place of "This is the letter", and is never the letter to catch (that one is asked by name).
- **x says /ks/ and ends words**: its keywords end with it ("like in fox"), and no word in the list begins with it (a test checks).
- **z, and s saying /z/**: asked for /z/, s is never one of the wrong choices, since from lesson 21 it says that too.
  Asked for /s/, z may be.

Our exercises against the eight steps:

| Exercise | Step it stands in for | Scored |
|---|---|---|
| `meet` | 5, the introduction only | no |
| `pick-letter` | 3 (tap in place of writing) | yes |
| `catch` | none directly: letter by *name*, at speed | light credit |
| `blend` | 4 for the sounding out, 6 for the rebuilding | the unaided stage only |
| `many` | 5 for lessons 20 and 21 | the ask stage |
| `heart` | 7: meet an irregular word, then pick it out by ear; `blend` spells it | picking it out, and spelling it unaided |
| `read` | 8, a first step: a phrase nobody says, tap the thing it names | yes |
| `chain` | 6: a word chain. One letter changes at a time, in place; spelling steps (the word is said, pick the letter that makes it) and reading steps (the letter changes by itself, nobody says the word, pick it out by ear) take turns | every step |

Word chains, as the game has them (`src/learn/chains.ts`, `src/exercises/chain.ts`):

- One chain a lesson, after the lesson's own rounds and before its irregular words; one at each checkpoint; one in a
  practice round that has word building in it. Four steps where the child's words link up that far, three if not,
  and none otherwise: with today's words the first is in lesson 6 (tap, map, mat, sat, pat), not lesson 5 as in UFLI,
  because am, at, mat and sat do not make a chain.
- A step only ever swaps one tile for another. UFLI's chains also add and drop letters (at to sat, ant to at); ours do not yet.
- Only words the child could be asked to build, every letter saying its usual sound: no "is" or "as", no irregular words.
- A spelling step is evidence for the letter put in and for the word's shape; a reading step for the shape. The
  letters used along the way are credited a little on a right answer, as in `blend`.
- A reading step is answered by ear: two or three speakers, a tap says a word and a second tap chooses it. Only nine
  words have drawings, so pictures could stand in for the speakers in few steps; one way of answering was kept.
- A lesson on new word shapes (25, 27) gets a chain of those shapes if its words link up at least twice: at 27 that is
  slip, skip, skin. The four-letter words in `words.json` hardly link (crab and grab, held and help, those three), so
  until more are added (drip, trap, clip, flap, slap would join them up) chains are almost all three-letter words.
- The exercise says three lines of its own (`CHAIN_PROMPTS`). Until all three are recorded no chain is offered at all.

What UFLI does that the game does not yet:

1. **Read a word nobody says** (steps 4, 6, 8). In `read`, for the nine drawn things, and in the reading steps of `chain`, for any word: but there the child matches it to a spoken word and never has to say it.
2. **Letter to sound** (step 2). Everything we have runs sound to letter.
3. **Word chains that add or drop a letter** (step 6). Chains that swap one are in (`chain`, above), once their lines are recorded.
4. **Sounds with no letters** (step 1): blending and segmenting by ear.
5. **Longer blends** (35 to 41). CCVC and CVCC are taught (25, 27); CCVCC and CCCVC are not, and `shapeOf` calls
   them `other`. The blend exercise already holds five tiles in both layouts.
6. **Heart words** (step 7). By lesson 27 UFLI has taught about twenty; the game has two, "the" and "a" (`docs/heart-words.md`).
7. **Sentences** (step 8). Phrases ("the cat") are read; whole sentences are made but not shown yet, for want of room.
8. **Letter formation** (step 5) and capital letters (Getting Ready). A decision to make, not an oversight to fix.
